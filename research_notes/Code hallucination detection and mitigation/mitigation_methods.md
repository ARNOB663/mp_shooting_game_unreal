## Q1. Execution-agreement / reranking selection methods (CodeT, MBR-exec, AlphaCode, self-consistency, LEVER, Coder-Reviewer, SRank, successors): pass@1 gains and compute

### Takeaway
Execution-based consensus selection is a mature, strong baseline family. CodeT (ICLR 2023) lifted code-davinci-002 HumanEval pass@1 from 47.0% to 65.8% (+18.8). SRank (ACL Findings 2024) adds about 3.6 points over CodeT on average. MPSC (ACL 2024) adds about 16 points over GPT-3.5 on HumanEval. 2025–2026 work (S*, Semantic Voting, symbolic-equivalence partitioning) keeps refining "cluster by behaviour on shared inputs, pick the dominant or most-overlapping cluster." PCV's "largest execution cluster" step is therefore essentially MBR-exec / AlphaCode clustering. Its novelty has to come from where the candidates come from (perturbed prompts) and from the abstention and escalation layer, not from the selection rule.

### Cited Findings
- **CodeT** (Chen et al., arXiv 2207.10397; ICLR 2023). It generates test cases with the same LLM, then scores solutions by "dual execution agreement": the number of generated tests a solution passes, combined with how many other solutions behave the same way. The design is RANSAC-inspired. — [ar5iv CodeT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.10397); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.10397)
- CodeT result: code-davinci-002 HumanEval pass@1 goes from **47.0% to 65.8% (+18.8 absolute)**, more than 20 points above the previous SOTA (zero-shot). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.10397); [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2207.10397)
- CodeT's gains are largest on easier benchmarks (HumanEval and MBPP). A 2026 paper (DuET) notes that HumanEval is near saturation, so headroom there is small. — [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/codet-code-generation-with-generated-tests); [DuET arXiv 2604.11514](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11514)
- CodeT compute: the sampling budget is on the order of 100 candidate programs per problem plus a separate batch of LLM-generated test cases. **[unverified this session: exact n]** — [arXiv 2207.10397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.10397)
- **SRank / Functional Overlap Reranking** (To, Nguyen, Bui; ACL Findings 2024; arXiv 2311.03366). It clusters solutions by identical execution outputs, builds an inter-cluster "interaction matrix" of functional overlap, and picks the cluster with the largest cumulative overlap. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.220/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03366); [GitHub](https://github.com/FSoft-AI4Code/SRank-CodeRanker)
- SRank HumanEval pass@1: **69.66% (Codex002), 75.31% (WizardCoder), 53.99% (StarCoder), 60.55% (CodeGen)**. That is about **6.1% average improvement over CodeT and Coder-Reviewer combined**. A secondary review splits this into about **3.63% over CodeT** and **8.81% over Coder-Reviewer**. — [ACL Anthology abstract](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.220/); [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/functional-overlap-reranking-for-neural-code-generation)
- **LEVER** (Ni et al., ICML 2023; arXiv 2302.08468). It trains a verifier on (NL, program, execution result), reranks by verifier score × LM probability, and marginalizes over programs with identical execution results. On MBPP the reported execution accuracy is **LEVER 75.4 vs MBR-Exec 63.0 vs Reviewer 66.9**. Removing execution results from the verifier input drops performance on all four benchmarks. LEVER needs a *trained* verifier, which makes it not training-free. — [arXiv html v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.08468v3); [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2023/poster/23632)
- **Coder-Reviewer reranking** (Zhang et al., ICML 2023; arXiv 2211.16490). It reranks by p(code|NL) × p(NL|code). The authors report "up to **17% absolute accuracy gain**" over Coder-only reranking. With executability filtering it "can often outperform" MBR-exec. On Codex002, MBR-Exec is more than 10% worse than normalized Coder-Reviewer on HumanEval. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16490); [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2023/poster/24463)
- **MBR-exec** (Shi et al., EMNLP 2022; arXiv 2204.11454) selects the sample whose execution outputs agree most with the other samples. DOCE (2024) finds that once trial-unit-test filtering is applied, likelihood features and Coder-Reviewer *underperform* MBR-Exec with filtering. Filtering gives the largest gain over random selection. Reported DOCE numbers: MBR-Exec 49.5 (HumanEval+) and 57.8 (MBPP-S+); Filter + CR 61.3 and 60.2. — [DOCE arXiv 2408.13745](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13745)
- **AlphaCode** (Li et al., Science 2022; arXiv 2203.07814). It samples up to about 1M programs per problem, filters on example tests, clusters by behaviour on model-generated inputs, and submits 10. It reached an average ranking of about the top 54.3% on Codeforces. **[unverified this session: 54.3% and 1M figures]** Large Language Monkeys notes that AlphaCode's solve rate scales log-linearly with samples and keeps improving up to a million samples. — [Large Language Monkeys PDF](https://scalingintelligence.stanford.edu/pubs/large_language_monkeys.pdf); [arXiv 2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)
- **MPSC: Multi-Perspective Self-Consistency** (Huang, Lu, Wan, Duan; ACL 2024; arXiv 2309.17272). It builds a graph over solutions, specifications and test cases, and scores by inter- and intra-perspective consistency. On GPT-3.5-Turbo the ACL version reports pass@1 gains of **+15.91% HumanEval, +15.64% HumanEval+, +6.43% MBPP, +9.37% CodeContests**; arXiv v1 reported +17.60 / +17.61 / +6.50 / +11.82. The GPT-3.5 baseline is 68.38 on HumanEval, and MPSC-Semantic reaches 83.35. A GPT-4 row shows 81.48 → 92.15 (+10.67) **[row attribution inferred by summarizer]**. — [ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.78.pdf); [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17272); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=hUs8YHAUEr)
- **S\*** (Li et al., EMNLP Findings 2025; arXiv 2502.14382) is hybrid test-time scaling: parallel sampling plus sequential debugging rounds, with selection by LLM-generated distinguishing test inputs ("adaptive input synthesis"). It was evaluated on 12 models (Qwen2.5-Coder 0.5B–32B, GPT-4o-mini, reasoning models) on LiveCodeBench and CodeContests. Reported gains are **+8.6 to +29.9 pp pass@1**. **Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct + S\* beats Qwen2.5-Coder-32B without S\* by 7%**. It outperforms majority voting and self-debugging. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.865.pdf); [NovaSky blog](https://novasky-ai.github.io/posts/S*/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14382)
- **EnsLLM** (arXiv 2503.15838, 2025) is a multi-LLM ensemble. It selects among candidates using pairwise CodeBLEU plus CrossHair-based behavioural (differential) comparison, and "consistently outperforms standalone LLMs" on HumanEval and LiveCodeBench. — [arXiv 2503.15838](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15838)
- **"Wisdom and Delusion of LLM Ensembles for Code Generation and Repair"** (arXiv 2510.21513, 2025). Favoring diverse candidates helps larger ensembles, and naive model pairing can hurt. — [arXiv 2510.21513](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21513)
- **Semantic Voting** (arXiv 2605.08680, 2026). The model writes abstract input categories, which are instantiated and used for execution-grounded consensus on HumanEval+ and MBPP+. Execution-based methods weaken as candidate diversity drops. — [arXiv 2605.08680](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08680)
- **Inference-Time Code Selection via Symbolic Equivalence Partitioning** (arXiv 2604.06485, 2026) is a successor to SRank that partitions candidates by symbolic equivalence rather than by outputs on sampled inputs. — [arXiv 2604.06485](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06485)
- **Known failure mode of output-clustering: "spurious consistency."** Wrong programs can produce identical outputs on the test inputs. One 2026 paper adds AST similarity to the clustering to counter this. Invalid LLM-generated test inputs can also split semantically identical programs into different clusters. — [arXiv 2603.27098](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27098); [arXiv 2605.17029](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17029)

### Inferences
- The baselines PCV must beat on selection are: (a) plain majority/MBR-exec over N i.i.d. samples from the original prompt, (b) CodeT, (c) SRank, and ideally (d) MPSC or S\*'s input-synthesis selection. All are training-free except LEVER. If PCV's selector is "largest execution cluster," it *is* MBR-exec / AlphaCode clustering. The ablation must show the gain comes from prompt perturbation (candidate diversity) rather than from the selector.
- Diversity matters for execution-consensus methods (Semantic Voting, Wisdom and Delusion). That supports PCV's hypothesis that perturbed prompts add useful diversity, but it also implies that perturbation lowering per-sample accuracy could hurt (see Q2).

### Gaps
- Exact sample counts per method (CodeT n=100? SRank n=?) could not be confirmed from primary text in this session.
- No direct 2025–2026 head-to-head table of CodeT, SRank, MPSC and S\* on the same ≤8B model was found.

## Q2. Prompt-ensemble / input-diversity methods: is perturbed-prompt ensembling for code new?

### Takeaway
Prompt-diversity ensembling is established for *reasoning/QA*: DiPPER (EMNLP 2025), SPUQ (EACL 2024), boosted/paraphrase prompt ensembles. Using rephrased queries to increase *diversity* for code sampling has been shown in at least one 2025 paper: random query rephrasing gave +6.5 to +13.7% pass@10 on HumanEval. A 2026 paper compares "typo sampling" with temperature sampling on HumanEval and finds typo sampling is *worse* than temperature sampling at matched budget. No paper found does the full PCV recipe (typo + synonym + paraphrase prompt ensemble, then execution-behaviour clustering, then majority cluster, then abstention) for code. The combination appears new, but each ingredient has close precedent, and the evidence that perturbation beats plain temperature sampling at equal compute is currently *negative*.

### Cited Findings
- **DiPPER** (Lau, Hu, Liu, Chen, Ng, Low; EMNLP 2025; arXiv 2412.15238) is a training-free single-LLM inference-time ensemble over an optimized, *diverse set of prompts*: a prompt generator, a diversity-based selector, and an aggregator. Three Qwen2-MATH-1.5B instances under DiPPER beat a 7B model on MATH. It covers reasoning, not code. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1801/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15238); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0wQsCNrlFl)
- **SPUQ** (Gao, Zhang, Mouatadid, Das; EACL 2024; arXiv 2403.02509). It perturbs inputs (paraphrasing via an external LLM, dummy tokens, temperature), samples per perturbation, and aggregates. This exposes epistemic uncertainty that repeated sampling alone misses. It reports **30–70% reduction in Expected Calibration Error**. It covers general NLP, not code. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.143.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02509); [GitHub](https://github.com/intuit-ai-research/SPUQ)
- **On the Effect of Sampling Diversity in Scaling LLM Inference** (arXiv 2502.11027, 2025). *Random query rephrasing* on HumanEval raised **Pass@10 over direct sampling by about 6.5%–13.7%**, depending on the model. This is the closest prior work to PCV's perturbed-prompt candidate generation for code, but it reports pass@k (oracle selection), not consensus selection or abstention. — [arXiv 2502.11027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11027)
- **Emergent retokenization symmetry** (arXiv 2606.15521, 2026). On HumanEval with OLMo-2-7B-Instruct, "typo sampling" (input-side perturbation) beats retokenization sampling but **stays below standard temperature sampling at every k**. Under a matched-budget analysis the ranking is temperature sampling > typo sampling > retokenization. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15521v1); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15521)
- **Prompt Variability Effects on LLM Code Generation** (arXiv 2506.10204, 2025). Synonym and paraphrase perturbations are a "much weaker way of corrupting the prompts" than typos, and LLMs are much more robust to them than to typos. — [arXiv 2506.10204](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10204v1)
- Prompt specificity alone (rewriting only the NL description of HumanEval prompts) changes code-generation results. — [arXiv 2508.03678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03678)
- Perturbed LeetCode-style prompts (storytelling reframing, irrelevant constraints) swing reasoning-model accuracy by about −42% / +35%. — [arXiv 2506.06971](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06971)
- Single-character prompt mutations can flip generated code from secure to vulnerable, across 3 models and 5 languages. — [arXiv 2605.29737](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29737)
- **Multi-Programming-Language Ensemble** (arXiv 2409.04114) uses diversity across target *languages* rather than prompts. — [arXiv 2409.04114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.04114)
- Asking for diverse code solutions over multiple prompting rounds yields solutions that are both diverse and accurate on HumanEval, with a diversity–correctness trade-off. — [arXiv 2403.13259](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13259)
- A survey of LLM ensembles notes that paraphrasing templates and permuting examples are used to diversify prompts. — [arXiv 2503.13505](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13505)
- Later work criticizes DiPPER-style single-sample evaluation: "given the substantial within-LLM variance, single-sample evaluations risk conflating sampling noise with genuine prompt or model effects." — [snippet via OpenReview DiPPER discussion / related](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0wQsCNrlFl)

### Inferences
- Novelty claim: "perturbed-prompt ensemble + execution-behaviour clustering + abstention for code" appears unoccupied in the literature found. However, the reviewers' obvious objection is already published: at equal sample budget, typo-perturbed prompts are *less* effective than temperature sampling (arXiv 2606.15521). PCV must show either (i) a gain in *selection accuracy / calibration* (SPUQ-style epistemic signal) rather than pass@k, or (ii) that paraphrase/synonym variants (which are less harmful than typos per 2506.10204) combined with temperature beat temperature alone at matched N.
- A strong design suggestion: use perturbation primarily as an *uncertainty signal* (cross-prompt disagreement), which is SPUQ's insight, and keep the original prompt dominant in the sample pool.

### Gaps
- "Rephrase and Respond" (Deng et al. 2023, arXiv 2311.04205) and ReCode (Wang et al., ACL 2023, arXiv 2212.10264) were not surfaced by the searches in this session. Their numbers are unverified. ReCode is a robustness *benchmark* for perturbed code prompts, not an ensemble method.
- No paper was found that measures cross-paraphrase *execution agreement* as a correctness predictor for code.

## Q3. API hallucination fixes: De-Hallucinator, documentation-augmented generation (CloudAPIBench), RAG for library APIs, constrained/monitor-guided decoding, package-hallucination mitigations

### Takeaway
Retrieval of API references and documentation helps mainly on *low-frequency* APIs and can *hurt* frequent ones unless retrieval is triggered selectively. CloudAPIBench's selective DAG gives +8.20% absolute for GPT-4o. Static-analysis-guided decoding (MGD) lets a 1.1B model beat text-davinci-003 on compilation rate (+16.5% relative). Package-hallucination rates are about 5.2% for commercial and 21.7% for open models, and RAG, self-feedback and fine-tuning reduce them. A 2026 decoding-time monitor (PackMonitor) targets zero package hallucinations. PCV's static API-existence verifier plus feedback is closest to De-Hallucinator's loop and to CloudAPIBench's "check against an API index" trigger.

### Cited Findings
- **De-Hallucinator** (Eghbali & Pradel; arXiv 2401.01701, 2024). It identifies project-specific API references related to the model's *initial* prediction, adds them to the prompt, and iterates, reusing model output as the retrieval query ("iterative prompt"). It was evaluated on Python API-usage prediction and JavaScript test generation. — [arXiv html v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.01701v3); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01701)
- De-Hallucinator headline numbers: edit-distance and API-recall improvements roughly in the 20–60% relative range for completion, and more passing tests for test generation. **[unverified this session: exact percentages; check abstract]** — [arXiv 2401.01701](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01701)
- De-Hallucinator latency overhead, as measured by MARIN (FSE 2025 companion, arXiv 2505.05057): **0.474s on APIHulBench-F and 0.482s on APIHulBench-M**. MARIN ran it for one iteration for a fair comparison. MARIN itself mitigates API hallucination with hierarchical dependency-aware constrained decoding. — [arXiv 2505.05057](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05057v1); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3696630.3728569)
- **CloudAPIBench / "On Mitigating Code LLM Hallucinations with API Documentation"** (Jain et al., Amazon; arXiv 2407.09726; IEEE, 2025). GPT-4o gets **78.67% valid high-frequency vs only 38.58% valid low-frequency** API invocations. DAG (documentation-augmented generation) raises low-frequency to **47.94%** but causes up to a **39.02% absolute drop on high-frequency APIs** with sub-optimal retrievers. The fix is to trigger retrieval selectively, either by checking against an **API index** or by using **model confidence**, which yields an **8.20% absolute improvement on CloudAPIBench for GPT-4o**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09726); [Amazon Science PDF](https://assets.amazon.science/8f/83/7407a5634a80a39e82b52ae935fe/on-mitigating-code-llm-hallucinations-with-api-documentation.pdf); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11121691/)
- **Monitor-Guided Decoding (MGD)** (Agrawal, Kanade, Goyal, Lahiri, Rajamani; NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2306.10763). A static-analysis monitor runs alongside decoding and masks tokens inconsistent with repository type and scope information. On PragmaticCode (Java), the metric is compilation rate after a clean build. All base models with MGD, including CodeGen-350M for k≥4, beat text-davinci-003. **SantaCoder-1.1B + MGD beats text-davinci-003's compilation rate by 16.55% relative**, and does so even with a budget of a single generation. — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/662b1774ba8845fc1fa3d1fc0177ceeb-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10763); [HF page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2306.10763)
- **Package hallucination**: "We Have a Package for You!" (Spracklen et al., USENIX Security 2025; arXiv 2406.10279) analyzed 576k code samples. The average package-hallucination rate is **≥5.2% for commercial and 21.7% for open-source models**. RAG, self-detected feedback and SFT all reduce hallucinations, with one model brought **below 3%**. The repo README says fine-tuning gave the largest reduction. (The often-quoted overall 19.7% could not be confirmed in this session's snippets.) — [USENIX PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity25/sec25cycle1-prepub-742-spracklen.pdf); [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10279v1); [GitHub](https://github.com/Spracks/PackageHallucination)
- **PackMonitor** (arXiv 2602.20717, 2026) claims "zero package hallucinations through decoding-time monitoring." This is an MGD-style constraint against a real package index. — [arXiv 2602.20717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20717)
- A study of Rust crate hallucination (arXiv 2606.08444, 2026) found self-refinement more consistently effective than RAG, with both giving modest gains. — [arXiv 2606.08444](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08444)
- RAG plus constrained decoding has also been applied to web-API invocation errors (arXiv 2607.05936, 2026). — [arXiv 2607.05936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05936)
- Systematic review of code-hallucination mitigation methods (arXiv 2511.00776). — [arXiv 2511.00776](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00776v1)

### Inferences
- PCV's static API-existence check (import or introspect against the real environment) is a *post-hoc* variant of MGD and PackMonitor (which act at decode time) and a *non-retrieval* variant of De-Hallucinator. Reviewers will ask why not constrain decoding. A post-hoc check is model-agnostic and works with quantized GGUF or black-box APIs, which is a defensible advantage.
- CloudAPIBench shows that *always* intervening harms frequent APIs. That supports PCV's adaptive escalation: only trigger repair when the verifier fires or agreement is low.

### Gaps
- Exact De-Hallucinator percentages and exact package-hallucination reductions per mitigation could not be verified from primary text here.
- No evaluation of API-existence verification specifically on ≤8B quantized models was found.

## Q4. Feedback / repair loops (Self-Debugging, Self-Refine, Reflexion, LDB, Olausson self-repair limits): gains, costs, small models

### Takeaway
With strong models and execution feedback, repair loops give +5 to +12 points: Self-Debugging up to +12%, LDB +8.4 on MBPP with GPT-3.5, Reflexion 91% HumanEval with GPT-4. Under compute-matched comparison against i.i.d. sampling, self-repair is often no better, particularly at small budgets and for weaker models (Olausson, ICLR 2024). For small (≤8B) open models, evidence is mixed to negative. A 2026 study reports that blind resampling beats self-repair for Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5–7B Q4_K_M. Reflexion showed no gain with a small model.

### Cited Findings
- **Self-Debugging** (Chen, Lin, Schärli, Zhou; ICLR 2024; arXiv 2304.05128). Few-shot "rubber-duck" self-debugging works without human feedback, with variants +Expl (line-by-line explanation) and +Trace (dry-run trace). — [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/2460396f2d0d421885997dd1612ac56b-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.05128)
- Self-Debugging headline: "improves baseline accuracy by up to 12%" on TransCoder and MBPP, and matches or beats baselines generating >10× more candidates. **[unverified this session]** — [arXiv 2304.05128](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.05128)
- **LDB** (Zhong et al., ACL Findings 2024; arXiv 2402.16906). It segments programs into basic blocks and feeds intermediate variable values to the LLM. MBPP with GPT-3.5 (baseline 67.6): Self-Debug+Expl 74.4 (+6.8), +Trace 72.6 (+5.0), **LDB 76.0 (+8.4)**. TransCoder with GPT-3.5: LDB +5.4 vs +3.6. CodeLlama-34B MBPP: LDB +6.2 vs +4.4/+6.0. **StarCoder-15B MBPP: LDB +3.8 vs +2.8/+3.2**. These are smaller gains for weaker models, and the Self-Debug baselines were reimplemented by the LDB authors. — [ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.49.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16906v1)
- VDebugger reports that SelfDebug and LDB *slightly hurt* performance in its visual-program setting, attributed to the limited self-debugging ability of small LLMs. — [arXiv 2406.13444](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13444)
- **Reflexion** (Shinn et al., NeurIPS 2023; arXiv 2303.11366): **91% pass@1 on HumanEval vs 80% for GPT-4**. — [NeurIPS poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/70114). A secondary, non-peer-reviewed blog says main results use GPT-4, that a small model (starchat-beta) showed **no improvement**, and that MBPP *dropped* from 80.1% to 77.1%. The tests used for self-evaluation are self-generated. — [beancount.io research log](https://beancount.io/bean-labs/research-logs/2026/04/25/reflexion-language-agents-verbal-reinforcement-learning) (verify against the paper)
- **Is Self-Repair a Silver Bullet?** (Olausson et al., ICLR 2024; arXiv 2306.09896). It compares repair against k i.i.d. samples, where k equals the total programs in the repair tree. "Pass rates are higher or equally high without repair in several instances, particularly when the budget is small." Repair is more likely to help when more budget goes to diverse *initial* samples. Example: GPT-4 on APPS with 10 initial samples plus one repair each goes from 63.1% to 66.1% vs pass@20 (about 1.05×). GPT-3.5 gains are marginal and appear only at the largest budgets, while GPT-4 beats the baseline by up to 8%. Under token-cost and sequential metrics, repair looks worse. — [ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9ddc141bdbf9d1db510cefff56c586ad-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.09896v5)
- **"Try Again, Don't Look Back: Blind Resampling Outperforms Self-Repair in Small Code Models"** (arXiv 2607.26117, 2026). Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5B/3B/7B Instruct, **Q4_K_M quantized**, on a single 6 GB consumer GPU. Blind resampling beats self-repair. — [arXiv 2607.26117](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26117)
- Qwen2.5-Coder-7B showed limited or negative gains from prompt-based self-debugging on HumanEval. A contrasting 2026 study ("How Many Tries Does It Take?", arXiv 2604.10508) argues prompt-based self-repair works across 7 models from 3 families, even at 8B. — [arXiv 2604.10508](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10508); [Inducing Code Change in LLM Self-Debugging (AI journal)](https://doi.org/10.3390/ai7100392)
- A 2026 preregistered placebo-controlled study on frozen small code models argues the value of repair feedback comes from *external, execution-grounded* criticism, not from re-exposing the model to its own failed output. — [arXiv 2606.31511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31511)
- Fine-tuning is the reliable route for small-model self-repair: CYCLE (350M–3B) and LeDex (SFT plus RL for CodeLlama-7B/13B, NeurIPS 2024). — [LeDex NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/3ea832724870c700f0a03c665572e2a9-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- "When Uncertainty Isn't Enough: An Empirical Study of Self-Correction in Code Generation" (arXiv 2608.14659, 2026) is relevant to uncertainty-gated repair. — [arXiv 2608.14659](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14659)

### Inferences
- For ≤8B and quantized models, PCV's design is consistent with the evidence. Spending budget on more diverse initial samples plus consensus selection is supported (Olausson; blind resampling paper). Repair should be driven by *external* deterministic signals, such as an API-existence error or a failing import, rather than free-form self-critique.
- Self-Refine (Madaan et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2303.17651) was not checked in this session, so no code numbers are quoted.

### Gaps
- Self-Refine's code-specific numbers are unverified.
- Exact effect sizes of the 2026 small-model repair papers (2607.26117, 2604.10508) were not available in the snippets.

## Q5. Selective generation / abstention for code (risk–coverage, conformal prediction, knowing when to abstain)

### Takeaway
This is the area closest to PCV's step (4), and it is active in 2025–2026. Sharma & David (2025) cluster programs via symbolic execution and abstain on high semantic entropy, reporting false-positive rate <0.02%. CodeRefuser (2026) abstains with execution-consistency clustering plus Learn-then-Test calibration (distribution-free guarantee), reporting +26.5% absolute over the best competitor. Ensemble Semantic Entropy (2026) reports +53.4% prediction accuracy under strict FPR. PCV's "abstain when cluster agreement is low" is essentially the cluster-ratio mode of CodeRefuser. To be novel it must add the *cross-prompt-perturbation* dimension and/or the API-verifier signal.

### Cited Findings
- **Sharma & David, "Assessing Correctness in LLM-Based Code Generation via Uncertainty Estimation"** (arXiv 2502.11620, 2025). It adapts entropy- and mutual-information-based NLG uncertainty to code, with **symbolic-execution-based clustering** of semantically equivalent programs. Uncertainty assuming a uniform distribution performs comparably, so token probabilities are not needed. Abstention policies achieve **false-positive rate below 0.02%**. The text also reports that embedding- or CodeBLEU-based semantic-entropy variants showed **no statistically significant correlation** with correctness, which motivates symbolic clustering. Versions disagree on wording: arXiv says "strong correlation," and a ResearchGate copy says "weak negative correlation." — [arXiv html v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11620v3); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11620)
- **Task Abstention for LLMs in Code Generation / CodeRefuser** (Zhou et al.; arXiv 2605.17029, 2026). It decides whether to refuse a coding task. The abstention rule uses *execution-outcome consistency* with no oracle tests. It is built on Learn-then-Test (calibration and test phases) with a **distribution-free guarantee**, and has two modes: a cluster-ratio threshold and semantic entropy over clusters. It filters invalid LLM-generated test inputs before clustering. It reports **26.5% absolute improvement on average over the best competitor** (exact metric unconfirmed). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17029); [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17029)
- **Ensemble-Based Uncertainty Estimation for Code Correctness** (arXiv 2603.27098, 2026). It computes Ensemble Semantic Entropy across several models in program-semantic space, clustering by identical execution signatures over shared inputs. On LiveCodeBench it correlates better with correctness than single-model SE, with **53.4% improvement in prediction accuracy under strict FPR constraints** in selective generation. It flags the spurious-consistency problem. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27098)
- Other 2026 code-uncertainty work: **Functional Entropy** (arXiv 2605.28500), **Code-MUE** (execution-based semantic interaction graphs, arXiv 2607.12273), and **semantic distance for uncertainty** (arXiv 2605.09023). — [arXiv 2605.28500](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.28500); [arXiv 2607.12273](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12273); [arXiv 2605.09023](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09023)
- **Conformal Prediction with Paraphrase-Aware Scoring** (arXiv 2610.04239, 2026) uses paraphrases inside conformal UQ for general LLMs. It is a direct conceptual neighbour of "perturbed prompt + abstention." — [arXiv 2610.04239](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04239v1)
- Uncertainty-guided decoding for code: **AdaDec** (entropy-triggered pause and rerank, arXiv 2506.08980) and **Uncertainty-Guided CoT for code** (arXiv 2503.15341). — [AdaDec](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08980v1); [UnCert-CoT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15341)
- Hidden-state probes predict code correctness or vulnerability before generation, with AUC about 0.75 and 0.67 (2026), and OPENIA probes final-layer states. — [arXiv 2605.29737](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29737)

### Inferences
- PCV's abstention component must be compared against CodeRefuser (cluster ratio + LTT) and Sharma & David (symbolic SE) on risk–coverage / AURC. Simply thresholding the largest-cluster fraction is already published. The defensible novelty is to use *agreement across perturbed prompts* as the uncertainty score (SPUQ insight transferred to execution clusters), and to show it gives better AURC than same-prompt cluster ratio at matched N.
- Calibrating the threshold with LTT or conformal methods would give a guarantee and match CodeRefuser's rigour.

### Gaps
- Exact risk–coverage numbers (coverage at fixed risk) for CodeRefuser and Sharma & David were not available in snippets.
- Classic conformal-for-code papers were not surfaced here and remain unchecked: Spiess et al., "Calibration and Correctness of LMs for Code" (ICSE 2025, arXiv 2402.02047), and conformal code-completion work.

## Q6. Which mitigations work for small open models (≤8B), and are there results for quantized models?

### Takeaway
Methods that rely on *external* signals (execution, static analysis, generated tests) work for small models: MGD on 350M–1.1B, S\* on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (beating 32B), and DiPPER on 1.5B. Methods that rely on the model's own critique (Reflexion, prompt-based self-debugging) degrade or vanish at small scale. Quantized-model evidence is thin. 4-bit typically keeps about 98–99% of HumanEval accuracy, and one 2026 study on Q4_K_M Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5–7B found blind resampling beats self-repair.

### Cited Findings
- MGD: CodeGen-350M and SantaCoder-1.1B with MGD beat text-davinci-003 on compilation rate. — [NeurIPS 2023 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/662b1774ba8845fc1fa3d1fc0177ceeb-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- S\*: Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct + S\* beats 32B without it by 7%. It was evaluated down to 0.5B. — [NovaSky](https://novasky-ai.github.io/posts/S*/); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.865.pdf)
- DiPPER: an ensemble of 3× Qwen2-MATH-1.5B beats a 7B model (math). — [arXiv 2412.15238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15238)
- LDB gains shrink with weaker models: GPT-3.5 +8.4, CodeLlama-34B +6.2, StarCoder-15B +3.8 on MBPP. — [ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.49.pdf)
- Reflexion: no improvement with a small model (starchat-beta), per a secondary source. — [beancount.io](https://beancount.io/bean-labs/research-logs/2026/04/25/reflexion-language-agents-verbal-reinforcement-learning)
- Blind resampling beats self-repair for Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5B/3B/7B, Q4_K_M. — [arXiv 2607.26117](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26117)
- Open-source models hallucinate packages far more (21.7%) than commercial ones (5.2%). — [USENIX 2025](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity25/sec25cycle1-prepub-742-spracklen.pdf)
- Quantization: an ACM study concludes "4-bit quantization is a safe bet" for code generation, with similar performance. Red Hat reports that 4-bit models recover about 98.9% of HumanEval accuracy. On low-resource languages (e.g., Lua), quantized models score below 50% (arXiv 2410.14766). — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3794763.3794810); [Red Hat](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2024/10/17/we-ran-over-half-million-evaluations-quantized-llms); [arXiv 2410.14766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14766)

### Inferences
- PCV's components are all external-signal or sampling-based (API verifier, execution clustering, perturbation), so they fit the regime where small models still benefit. Targeting ≤8B quantized models is a real gap in the literature.

### Gaps
- No study measuring *API/package hallucination rates* specifically under 4-bit quantization was found.

## Q7. How to do compute-fair comparisons (equal generations / tokens)

### Takeaway
Use Olausson's protocol: compare against i.i.d. sampling with the same total number of programs, and also report token cost. Count every LLM call (perturbation generation, test generation, repair, reviewer scoring) in the budget. Report ranked pass@1 with the candidate-pool size stated. Recent work normalizes to "forward-equivalent units" and fixes everything except the selection step. Adaptive-compute work (ICLR 2025) reports the same accuracy with up to 50% less compute. That is the template for PCV's adaptive escalation claim.

### Cited Findings
- Olausson et al. compare self-repair against k i.i.d. samples, where k equals the total programs in the repair tree, and also consider token-cost and sequential metrics. Under those metrics repair looks less favourable. — [ICLR 2024 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9ddc141bdbf9d1db510cefff56c586ad-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Raw pass@k curves compare at equal *samples* rather than equal *prompt budgets*. A matched-budget analysis reverses rankings: temperature sampling > typo sampling > retokenization. — [arXiv 2606.15521](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15521)
- Normalize methods to forward-equivalent units, charging N generation passes plus any scoring pass. — [arXiv 2607.09693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09693)
- An equal-budget protocol for code: same number of rollouts (320), temperature, token limit and checkpoint, so only the selection step varies (Surprisal-Guided Selection, arXiv 2602.07670). — [arXiv 2602.07670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07670)
- **"Learning How Hard to Think"** (ICLR 2025): the budget B is the average samples per query, and the matched baseline is Best-of-k with k = B. It reports the same performance with **up to 50% less compute** on Math and Code. — [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/ff414825df833edb8b1839e3d5d495e9-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Ranked pass@k: whether any of the top-k selected programs passes, from a larger stated pool (Lightweight reranking, ACL 2024). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.376.pdf)
- Adaptive self-consistency methods typically use frequency or entropy stopping rules, which may be uncalibrated (CAS, arXiv 2607.01612). — [arXiv 2607.01612](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01612)
- Large Language Monkeys: coverage scales log-linearly over four orders of magnitude of samples, and the gap between coverage and *selection* is the bottleneck without a perfect verifier (SWE-bench Lite 15.9% → 56% with repeated sampling). — [arXiv 2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)

### Inferences
- Recommended protocol for PCV:
  - (i) Fix N total generated programs (e.g., N ∈ {5, 10, 20}) and compare PCV (N split across perturbed prompts) against MBR-exec, CodeT and SRank on N i.i.d. samples from the original prompt.
  - (ii) Charge the paraphrase-generation calls and any repair calls to PCV's budget.
  - (iii) Report average samples per query for the adaptive version against fixed-N baselines, as a pass@1-vs-compute curve.
  - (iv) Report AURC / risk–coverage for abstention against CodeRefuser-style cluster-ratio on same-prompt samples.
  - (v) Report the shared-input test set's provenance, because LLM-generated inputs cause spurious splits and merges.

### Gaps
- Adaptive-Consistency (Aggarwal et al., EMNLP 2023) and Early-Stopping Self-Consistency (ICLR 2024) sample-savings numbers were not verified in this session.
